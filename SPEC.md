## 1. Vision & design pillars

**One sentence:** A huge, fully 3D, first-person Japanese-inspired land rendered like a
moving pixel-art painting, made for wandering, discovering and feeling at peace.

**The feeling I want:** Standing on a wooden dock at night. Strings of warm lanterns sway
above you. The lake is a dark mirror with a shimmering golden path of reflected light.
A rowboat bumps gently against a post. Fireflies drift over the reeds. Somewhere a wind
chime rings. In the distance a mountain fades into blue haze. Nothing is in a hurry.

**Reference images** (in `reference/night-dock.webp` and `reference/mirror-lake.jpg`): (1) a lakeside boathouse at night, with lantern
strings, a wooden pier, a rowboat, deep navy water with golden reflections and autumn
leaves; (2) a mirror-calm mountain lake at dusk with autumn forests, a boat leaving a
white wake, and pastel mountain layers. Both are pixel art with painterly clusters,
soft glow and rich reflections. **That is the target, in full 3D.**

### Pillars (every feature must serve at least one)
1. **Atmosphere first.** Light, weather, sound and motion matter more than mechanics.
2. **Everything is alive.** Something is always moving: grass, leaves, birds, smoke,
   water, people, insects, clouds.
3. **Curiosity is the only goal.** No combat, no fail states, no grind. The reward for
   exploring is beauty, a secret, a story or a kind word.
4. **Hand-crafted feel at huge scale.** Procedural systems, but directed by authored
   rules, landmarks and composition so nothing looks random or repetitive.
5. **Calm, never empty.** Quiet moments are intentional; dead zones are not.

### Hard NOs
- No weapons, combat, enemies, damage, health, stamina, hunger, thirst, temperature
  survival, timers, fail states, or death.
- No crafting trees, no grinding, no currency economy (a tiny barter/gift system is ok).
- No multiplayer, accounts, ads, purchases, analytics, or network calls at runtime.
- No copying of characters, maps, music or assets from existing games or artists.
  Everything original and generated in code.

## 2. Platform, tech stack & project architecture

### Platform
- **Main target: my iPad, fully offline.** Browser game as an installable **PWA**:
  open it once in Safari, "Add to Home Screen", and from then on it runs from the home
  screen icon **with no internet at all** (airplane mode must work).
- Primary targets: **iPad Air M1 (Safari, iPadOS 17+)** with touch, and **desktop
  Chrome/Safari/Firefox** with keyboard + mouse. Gamepad via the Gamepad API.

### Offline on iPad (hard requirement)
- Zero network requests at runtime. No CDNs, no web fonts from servers, no analytics,
  no external assets. Everything (code, shaders, fonts, generated textures, audio) is
  bundled or generated in code.
- **Service worker precaches every file** of the build (precache manifest generated
  at build time, e.g. Workbox via `vite-plugin-pwa`, or a hand-written one listing
  all hashed assets). Cache-first strategy. App must cold-start in airplane mode.
- Web app manifest: `display: "fullscreen"` (fallback `standalone`), landscape
  orientation, theme/background colours from the night palette, full icon set
  including `apple-touch-icon` (180×180) and a pixel-art lantern icon generated by a
  script. iOS meta tags: `apple-mobile-web-app-capable`,
  `apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style = black-translucent`, splash/launch images
  for iPad sizes.
- Call `navigator.storage.persist()` so iPadOS doesn't evict saves; show a gentle
  warning in settings if persistence is not granted, and offer save export.
- **Updates:** when a new version has been cached, show a small pixel toast
  "A new version is ready — restart to update". Never force-reload mid-game.
- Keep the total download reasonable (target < 30 MB) so the first install is fast;
  generate heavy things (textures, impostors, audio buffers) on device at first launch
  and cache them in IndexedDB for faster later starts.
- Handle iPad lifecycle: pause on `visibilitychange`/`pagehide`, save immediately,
  resume audio context on the next touch (iOS requires a user gesture), handle WebGL
  context loss and restore gracefully.
- Prevent all browser behaviours: pinch zoom, double-tap zoom, pull-to-refresh,
  text selection, long-press callouts, rubber-band scrolling.
- **Hosting for the one-time install:** deploy the build to **GitHub Pages** via a
  GitHub Actions workflow (correct `base` path in Vite). Document in the README:
  "open this URL on the iPad once → Share → Add to Home Screen → done, works offline".
- Test offline: Playwright test that loads the app, goes offline, reloads, and checks
  that the game boots and a save loads.
- Landscape orientation. Handle safe areas (notch, home indicator).
- Performance target: **60 fps on iPad Air M1 at "Medium"**, 60 fps on a mid-range
  laptop at "High". Never below 30 fps on "Low".

### Stack
- **TypeScript (strict)**, **Vite**, **Three.js** (WebGL2). Custom GLSL shaders where
  needed. No heavy engine on top.
- Web Workers for terrain generation, meshing, vegetation scattering and pathfinding.
- Web Audio API for all sound (procedural synthesis + generated sample buffers).
- IndexedDB for saves (via a small wrapper), localStorage only for settings.
- Service worker for offline caching (Vite PWA plugin or hand-written).
- Seeded PRNG everywhere (e.g. mulberry32/xoshiro). **Same seed = same world.**
- Noise: simplex/OpenSimplex 2D/3D, implemented locally.
- No runtime CDNs. All dependencies bundled.

### Architecture rules
- Systems communicate through a small typed **event bus** and a shared **world clock**.
- Everything that can be pooled (particles, NPC meshes, sounds) is pooled.
- Content (plants, animals, NPCs, dialogue, secrets, items) lives in **data files**
  (TS objects) separate from logic, so it is easy to add more.
- A **debug panel** (toggle with `` ` `` or a 3-finger tap) from day one: FPS, draw
  calls, triangles, chunk count, time of day slider, weather picker, season picker,
  teleport to region/landmark, free-fly camera, toggle each post-FX pass, seed input.

## 3. The look: rendering pipeline (MOST IMPORTANT)

The goal is **3D pixel-art painting**: real geometry, real lights and shadows, but every
frame looks like it was hand-pixelled by an artist. Not "Minecraft blocks". Not "a
blurry low-res game". Crisp, deliberate pixels with painterly colour clusters, soft
glows and rich reflections.

### 3.1 Render resolution & upscaling
- Render the 3D scene into a **low-res render target**. Default internal height
  **360 px** (setting: 240 / 300 / 360 / 450 / 540). Width follows the aspect ratio
  and is rounded to an integer.
- Upscale to the screen with **nearest-neighbour** filtering, using an **integer scale**
  where possible; otherwise letterbox minimally or use a sharp-bilinear shader
  (integer pre-scale + bilinear) so pixels stay square and even.
- The UI is **also pixel art** (see §9.4): drawn at its own integer-scaled pixel grid
  so UI pixels are crisp and the same size everywhere, matching the game world.

### 3.2 Pixel stability (no shimmer while moving)
- Snap the camera position to the world-space size of one texel in the view direction
  where feasible, and apply the sub-pixel remainder as an offset in the final upscale
  pass (the "smooth pixel camera" technique), so the image stays stable but motion is
  still smooth.
- Shadow map texel snapping (stabilised cascades).
- Textures use point sampling with mipmaps chosen carefully; avoid high-frequency
  detail that turns into noise at low resolution.
- Vegetation uses alpha-tested cutouts (not alpha-blended) with dithered fade for LOD.

### 3.3 Lighting model
- **Stylised diffuse:** compute normal Lambert, then remap through a soft **3–5 band
  ramp** (configurable per region/time) so light falls in painterly steps, not smooth
  gradients. Keep a very soft blend between bands to avoid harsh posterization.
- **Hemispheric ambient** from sky colour (top) and ground bounce colour (bottom),
  both driven by the time-of-day palette.
- **Sun/moon directional light** with **cascaded shadow maps** (2–3 cascades,
  stabilised). Shadow edges are crisp at pixel scale. Shadows tinted (cool blue/purple
  in daylight, never pure black).
- **Many point lights** (lanterns, windows, hearths, fireflies, candles): use a
  **clustered or tiled forward** approach or a capped "nearest N lights" per object,
  since a village at night may have 100+ lanterns. Far lanterns become emissive sprites
  with glow only.
- **Emissive surfaces** (paper lanterns, shōji screens lit from inside, windows) glow
  warmly and feed bloom.
- **Rim light** on foliage and characters at sunrise/sunset for that golden edge.
- **Subsurface fake** on leaves and paper (light shines through when backlit).
- **Ambient occlusion:** baked per-vertex AO on terrain and buildings + cheap SSAO at
  low res on High.

### 3.4 Colour pipeline
1. Linear HDR lighting.
2. Tone mapping (ACES-like or a custom filmic curve), tuned to keep nights deep blue
   rather than grey.
3. **Colour grade per time of day and weather** via a 3D LUT (generated in code) or
   lift/gamma/gain + split toning.
4. **Palette quantization:** map each pixel to the nearest colour in the active
   palette (perceptual distance in OKLab). Palettes have 48–96 colours and are
   **blended between times of day**.
5. **Ordered dithering** (Bayer 4×4 or blue noise) between the two nearest palette
   colours, at low strength (setting: 0–100%). Stronger in skies/gradients, weaker on
   textured surfaces.
6. Optional **film grain** extremely subtle.

### 3.5 Glow, bloom & haze
- Low-res **bloom** on emissives and bright highlights, with a slightly larger, softer
  halo around lanterns (like the reference image: glowing orbs with a dithered halo).
- **Lantern halo sprites** around each lamp, dithered, pulsing very slightly (flame
  flicker).
- **Height fog** + **distance fog** with colour from the sky gradient. Valleys hold
  denser mist in the morning.
- **Aerial perspective:** distant mountains shift towards sky colour in stepped
  layers, giving the classic pastel mountain layering.
- **Light shafts (god rays):** screen-space radial blur from the sun when visible +
  geometric light-shaft cards in forests and through windows/shōji into interiors.

### 3.6 Outlines (subtle, optional)
- Depth/normal-based **1-pixel outline** pass, coloured as a darker shade of the
  surface (not black), applied mainly to buildings, characters, boats and props.
- Off by default for terrain and grass. Setting: Off / Subtle / Strong.

### 3.7 Sky
- Physically inspired **gradient sky** (Rayleigh/Mie approximation) remapped into the
  palette. Distinct sunrise, morning, noon, golden hour, sunset, blue hour, night.
- **Sun** and **moon** discs drawn as pixel sprites with a halo. Moon has 8 phases and
  lights the scene noticeably more when full.
- **Stars** as crisp single pixels with twinkle, constellations (original, from the lore),
  a faint **Milky Way** band, and occasional **shooting stars**.
- **Clouds:** layered 2D volumetric-ish clouds (raymarched at very low res, or layered
  noise sprites) with lit edges at sunset; they cast **moving cloud shadows** on the
  terrain via a projected noise texture.
- **Aurora-like "spirit lights"** on rare winter nights (see §23).

### 3.8 Water (star feature)
- **Planar reflections** for lakes (one reflection camera per visible water height,
  max 2 at a time, rendered at even lower res), mixed with screen-space reflection
  fallback and a sky-colour fallback in the distance.
- **Mirror calm** on windless mornings/evenings: reflections almost perfect, like the
  second reference image.
- Reflections broken into **horizontal pixel streaks** by wave normals; lanterns and the
  moon create a **glitter path** of golden pixels (reference image 1).
- **Waves:** sum of Gerstner/sine waves driven by wind; ripples from rain drops,
  fish, frogs, boats, the player wading, thrown pebbles.
- **Boat wakes:** V-shaped wake + white foam trail (reference image 2).
- **Depth colour:** shallow water shows sand/rocks/plants (refraction with depth
  fade); deep water goes dark blue/green.
- **Shore foam** line, gentle lapping animation, wet sand darkening.
- **Caustics** projected on shallow lake/river floors on sunny days.
- **Rivers & streams** with flow maps, small rapids, white water around rocks.
- **Waterfalls** as animated scrolling meshes + mist particles + rainbow in sunlight.
- **Ocean** on the coast with bigger swells and crashing waves at cliffs.
- **Puddles** after rain that reflect lanterns and sky.
- **Ice** on winter lakes in the north (walkable, with cracks and trapped bubbles).

### 3.9 Terrain shading
- Tri-planar mapping with procedural **pixel-art textures** (generated at 32–64 px,
  palette-friendly clusters): grass, moss, soil, mud, gravel, sand, rock (several),
  snow, path dirt, stone paving, rice paddy mud.
- Blend between materials by slope, height, moisture, region and paths, using
  **dithered blending** (not smooth alpha) for a pixel-art feel.
- Grass colour tinted by season and region; dry patches, flower patches.

### 3.10 Vegetation rendering
- **GPU instancing** for everything. Grass as dense cross/billboard cards with
  palette-friendly texture clusters, shaded with the terrain normal (so fields look
  like soft painted strokes, not spiky lines).
- Trees: trunk/branch meshes + **leaf cluster cards** (like painterly blobs) with
  wind animation (trunk sway, branch sway, leaf flutter, all driven by a global wind
  field so you see gusts travelling).
- LOD: full mesh → simplified mesh → impostor billboard (pre-rendered at load, with
  normals for lighting).
- Interaction: grass bends away from the player, animals and NPCs (trail texture).

### 3.11 Interiors
- Interiors use the same pixel pipeline, but warmer palettes, darker ambient,
  strong **window light shafts**, dust motes, hearth/candle flicker.
- Seamless transitions: doors and shōji slide open; you walk in without a loading
  screen (interiors are part of the building mesh, streamed with it).

### 3.12 Post-FX order (reference)
```
scene (low-res HDR) → SSAO (High) → fog/aerial → light shafts → bloom
→ tonemap → colour grade/LUT → palette quantize + dither → outlines
→ upscale (pixel-stable) → vignette (subtle) → UI
```

## 4. Art direction rules & colour palettes

### Rules
- Think like a pixel artist: **clusters, not noise**. Large shapes, readable silhouettes,
  limited palettes, deliberate highlights.
- **Warm lights vs cool shadows** is the core contrast (amber lanterns on navy night,
  golden sun on purple shadows).
- **Every scene needs a focal light** at night (a lantern, a window, a fire, the moon).
- Saturation is moderate; reserve the most saturated colours for lanterns, autumn
  maples, cherry blossoms, torii gates and festival details.
- Silhouettes of Japanese architecture (curved roofs, torii, pagodas) should read
  clearly against the sky.
- Avoid pure black and pure white.

### Time-of-day palettes (starting points; tune them)
- **Night (reference 1):** deep navy `#0e1430`, indigo `#1c2550`, slate blue `#33406b`,
  moon-blue highlight `#7d8fc2`, lantern amber `#ffb347`, lantern core `#ffe6a3`,
  ember orange `#e2622b`, warm wood in light `#8a4f2a`, wood in shadow `#2b1e24`.
- **Dusk / golden hour (reference 2):** lavender `#9c8fc4`, dusty pink `#d9a0a8`,
  peach `#f2b38a`, autumn red `#c2452f`, golden yellow `#e8b84a`, pine green `#2f5a4a`,
  mountain blue `#4a5a8a`, mirror-lake pale `#c9c3e0`.
- **Morning:** misty teal `#7fa8a8`, pale mint `#c8e0cf`, soft gold `#f0d79a`,
  fresh green `#6fa35a`, pearl white `#eef0e6`.
- **Noon:** sky blue `#79b4e0`, leaf green `#5c9a48`, warm stone `#b8a68a`,
  roof tile grey-blue `#5a6478`, vermilion `#d0452e`.
- **Rain/overcast:** desaturated blue-greens, wet dark wood, lanterns become more
  important.
- **Winter:** snow whites tinted blue/lilac, black-green pines, red accents.

Each region can tint the palette slightly (e.g. bamboo forest greener, ashen pass
greyer, cherry hills pinker).

## 5. World structure & generation

### Scale
- World about **10 km × 10 km** playable (bigger if performance allows), surrounded by
  sea on one side and impassable but beautiful mountain ranges on the others (visible
  far beyond the playable border as distant layered silhouettes).
- Walking from one edge to the other should take ~15–20 minutes; rowing across the
  big lake ~3 minutes.

### Generation approach ("directed procedural")
1. **Region map:** an authored layout of regions (a rough map in code: centre points,
   shapes, priorities) distorted with noise so borders are organic. Blend zones of
   100–300 m between regions.
2. **Heightfield:** multi-octave noise with domain warping + ridged noise for mountains,
   shaped per region (rolling hills, terraces, steep cliffs, valleys, plateaus).
3. **Landmarks first:** place key landmarks (Lantern Lake, the castle on its mountain,
   the great waterfall, the temple stair, villages) and then **sculpt terrain around
   them** (flatten village sites, carve the lake basin, raise the castle hill).
4. **Hydrology:** simulate rivers from mountain sources down to lakes and the sea
   (simple flow accumulation on the heightfield), carve riverbeds, place waterfalls at
   steep drops, ponds in hollows.
5. **Road & path network:** connect villages and landmarks using A* over a slope-cost
   map; main roads (gravel, stone lanterns along them), side paths (dirt), hidden trails
   (barely visible), shrine stairs (stone steps, torii tunnels). Bridges where roads
   cross rivers.
6. **Scatter:** plants, rocks, props, animals using Poisson-disc sampling with rules
   by region, slope, moisture, height, distance to water/roads/buildings.
7. **Composition pass:** guarantee "views": framing trees at viewpoints, clear lines of
   sight to landmarks (e.g. the castle visible from many places as a navigation
   anchor), no ugly prop collisions.

### Streaming
- Chunk size e.g. **64 m**, generated in workers, meshed with **LOD rings** (full near,
  reduced mid, very low far), skirts/stitching to hide seams.
- **Far terrain** as a single low-poly world mesh so mountains on the horizon are
  always visible.
- Generation must never stall the main thread; budget per frame for uploads.
- Deterministic: chunks regenerate identically; world changes (discoveries, opened
  doors, moved objects) stored as deltas in the save.

## 6. Regions (detailed)

For each region: terrain, flora, fauna, buildings, sounds, palette tint, secrets.

### 6.1 Lantern Lake (heart of the world, starting area)
- Large lake (~1.5 km) in a basin surrounded by gentle hills and maple forest.
- **Lake village "Tomoshibi"** on stilts and docks: boathouses, a lantern maker's
  workshop, a tea house over the water, strings of lanterns between poles (like
  reference 1), rowboats moored, fishing nets drying.
- Small islands: one with a tiny shrine and a single old pine, one with a stone
  lantern only visible at low mist, one with a ruined pavilion.
- Fauna: koi near docks, ducks, herons, kingfishers, otters, frogs, dragonflies,
  fireflies over the reed belts in summer.
- Sounds: water lapping on wood, creaking boats, wind chimes, frogs, distant bells.
- Secrets: a sunken torii visible from a boat on clear days; a lantern that lights by
  itself on new-moon nights; a letter in a bottle in the reeds.

### 6.2 Rice-Terrace Valley "Inaho"
- Wide valley with stepped rice terraces climbing both slopes, water channels, small
  wooden water wheels, scarecrows (kakashi), stone walls.
- Farming village: farmhouses with thick thatched (kayabuki) roofs, storehouses,
  persimmons drying under eaves, a small mill.
- Seasons change dramatically: flooded mirror-terraces in spring (reflecting sky),
  green in summer, golden in autumn with harvesting villagers, snow-covered steps in
  winter.
- Fauna: frogs, egrets, sparrows, crows, tanuki at night, dragonflies, a shiba dog.
- Secrets: a tiny shrine to the rice spirit hidden in the highest terrace; a scarecrow
  that faces a different direction every day (and always points to something).

### 6.3 Bamboo Forest "Sasayabu"
- Tall, dense bamboo with narrow paths, light falling in vertical shafts, stone
  steps covered in fallen leaves, a quiet temple deep inside.
- Bamboo creaks and knocks in wind; sounds are muffled; light is green.
- Fauna: sparrows, a shy serow, butterflies, beetles, a white snake (rare, lucky).
- Secrets: a clearing where the bamboo grows in a perfect circle; a flute left on a
  stone that plays a melody you can learn.

### 6.4 Autumn Maple Mountains "Momijiyama"
- Steep mountains with red/orange/gold maple forests (always autumn-tinted, extra
  vivid in autumn), waterfalls, rope and wooden bridges across gorges, mountain shrine.
- **The Great Waterfall:** huge cascade into a misty pool, with a hidden cave behind it.
- Fauna: macaques, deer, kites, crows, squirrels.
- Secrets: the cave behind the waterfall; a hermit's hut; a cliffside viewpoint over
  Lantern Lake.

### 6.5 Cherry-Blossom Hills "Sakuraoka"
- Soft rolling hills with groves of cherry trees, a picnic meadow, a small shrine,
  a long path lined with trees forming a pink tunnel.
- In spring: blossoms everywhere, petals drifting across the land, petals floating on
  ponds. Rest of the year: lush green, then red leaves, then bare branches with snow.
- Fauna: bush warblers (uguisu song), white-eyes, butterflies, rabbits.
- Secrets: the oldest cherry tree (giant, propped up with wooden crutches, roped with
  shimenawa); blossoms at night glow faintly on one special night.

### 6.6 Misty Cedar Forest "Kirisugi"
- Ancient giant cedars, moss-covered ground, ferns, roots, stone lanterns and Jizō
  statues with red bibs along an old pilgrimage path, thick mist, little sunlight.
- Very quiet; occasional drip, crow, distant bell.
- Fauna: owls, foxes, deer, mushrooms glowing faintly at night, fireflies by streams.
- Secrets: a forgotten shrine with a fox statue missing its pair; a path of a thousand
  small torii; a tree with a door carved into it.

### 6.7 Coastal Cliffs & Fishing Harbour "Shiomachi"
- Rugged coast, pine trees bent by wind, rock arches, a sandy cove, tide pools.
- Fishing harbour: wooden piers, fishing boats, net sheds, drying fish and seaweed,
  a lighthouse (stone + wooden, lit at night, sweeping beam), a small market.
- Tides: low tide reveals tide pools and a sunken shrine.
- Fauna: gulls, cormorants, crabs, sea otters, jellyfish glowing at night, dolphins
  sometimes in the distance, a whale breaching very rarely.
- Secrets: the "wedded rocks" joined by a rope; a cave only reachable at low tide.

### 6.8 Snowy High Pass & Onsen Town "Yukemuri"
- High mountain pass with permanent snow up high, pine and birch, frozen waterfall.
- **Onsen town:** steaming hot springs, wooden inns (ryokan), paper lanterns in snow,
  a footbath on the main street, steam rising from drains.
- Fauna: snow monkeys bathing in an outdoor spring, ptarmigans, a white fox, ravens.
- Secrets: a hidden outdoor bath high on the mountain with a perfect view; an ice cave
  with blue light.

### 6.9 Reed Marsh "Ashihara"
- Flat wetlands with reeds, boardwalk paths, lotus ponds, a lonely fisher's hut,
  wooden duck blinds, half-sunken boats.
- At summer night: the **biggest firefly gathering** in the world. Sound of frogs.
- Fauna: herons, cranes (dancing in winter), frogs, turtles, dragonflies, eels.
- Secrets: an island reachable only by stepping stones that appear at low water;
  a lotus that blooms only at dawn.

### 6.10 The Old Castle "Kagejō" (on a mountain above the lake)
- Ruined castle: massive curved stone walls, gatehouses, broken stairs, overgrown
  courtyards, a collapsed armoury now full of flowers, a keep (tenshu) mostly intact,
  with 5 floors you can climb to a viewing balcony.
- Atmosphere: wind whistling, crows, ivy, moss, a pond in the inner garden with koi
  descendants still alive.
- Story heart of the lore (§19). Rooms show traces of the past (scrolls, a tea room,
  a child's toy, a broken shamisen).
- Secrets: the secret garden behind a hidden gate, a hidden room in the keep,
  echoes of the past at certain times (§23).

### 6.11 Market Town "Ichiba" (largest village)
- Crossroads town with a main street of shops (closed/open by schedule): sweets,
  pottery, cloth, lanterns, tea, umbrellas, noodles. A big shrine with a festival
  ground, a stage, a bell tower, a well, an inn.
- Busiest place: most NPCs, children, cats, merchants calling out (in soft voice
  sounds), banners and noren curtains swaying.

### 6.12 Mountain Temple "Kumoji"
- Temple complex reached by a **very long stone stairway** through the forest
  (hundreds of steps, with rest spots), a pagoda (5-storey), a bell, a rock garden,
  monks sweeping, a view above the clouds on misty mornings ("sea of clouds").

### 6.13 Minor places (dozens, spread everywhere)
Lonely farmhouses, roadside tea stalls, wayside shrines, abandoned mills, charcoal
burners' huts, a fire-watch tower, a stone circle of Jizō, old bridges, a shipwreck,
a meadow of pampas grass (susuki) that glows silver in moonlight, a lone pine on a
cliff, a sunflower field, a lavender-like field, hot spring puddles in a forest,
a hermit's cave, a wind-bell tree, a fishing platform, a wishing well.

### 6.14 More regions
- **Ginkgo Plateau "Ichōdai":** a high, windy plateau with huge ancient ginkgo trees.
  In autumn the ground turns into a carpet of pure gold, and leaves fall all at once on
  one special day. There's an abandoned school house with a bell, and grazing horses.
  Secret: the oldest ginkgo has a hollow with a sleeping owl and a child's time capsule.
- **Wisteria Gorge "Fujikawa":** a narrow river gorge with wisteria hanging from both
  cliffs like purple curtains, wooden plank walkways bolted to the rock, little rope
  ferries, kingfishers and a tiny gorge-side inn. Secret: a walkway that ends in a
  hidden balcony behind the wisteria.
- **Hydrangea Hills "Ajisaiyama":** a temple hillside covered with blue, violet and
  pink hydrangeas, beautiful in the rainy season (early summer): snails, frogs on
  leaves, villagers walking with umbrellas. Secret: one hydrangea bush blooms white
  and moves to a different spot each year.
- **Tea Slopes "Chabatake":** rows of round tea bushes over rolling hills, a tea
  factory with a steaming roof, pickers in wide hats, fans on poles against frost,
  a tea house with a view of a distant volcano. Secret: an ancient wild tea tree in
  the forest above.
- **The Quiet Volcano "Hinoyama":** a sleeping volcano on the horizon with a faint
  plume of steam. Its lower slopes have black sand, sulphur-yellow vents, steaming
  pools that boil eggs, wind-sculpted pines and a weather station hut. The crater rim
  has a turquoise crater lake and a shrine. Calm and safe, no eruptions; the ground
  is only warm.
- **Sunflower & Cosmos Fields "Hanano":** summer sunflowers taller than you, autumn
  cosmos in pink and white, a windmill-like water pump, a dirt road with a single bus
  stop sign (no bus ever, it's a running joke for villagers), a tiny shop selling
  shaved ice in summer.
- **Island of the Gulls "Kamomejima":** a small island off the coast, reached by the
  ferry or your rowboat on calm days, with a fishing hamlet, a cat colony (more cats
  than people), a lighthouse keeper and a cave shrine. Secret: the hidden cat village.
- **Mirror Lakes "Kagami-ike":** five small, still mountain lakes at different heights
  connected by streams. Each reflects the landscape perfectly at dawn; the highest
  reflects a different season's sky (§20).
- **The Pampas Moor "Susukihara":** wide silver pampas grass moorland that waves like
  the sea in wind and glows in moonlight; a lonely stone path, a broken signpost,
  rabbits, harvest moon viewing spot.
- **The Old Pilgrim Road "Kumanomichi":** a long mountain trail linking the temple,
  the cedar forest and the volcano, with stone steps worn into grooves, waystation
  huts, tea stalls, little Jizō shrines every few hundred metres, and stamp posts.

### 6.15 Geography & macro composition
- **Layout (rough):** Lantern Lake in the centre. The Castle mountain on the lake's
  north shore, visible from almost everywhere as the navigation anchor. The Maple
  Mountains and the Great Waterfall to the north-west. The Snowy Pass and the volcano
  in the far north. The Cedar Forest and Bamboo Forest to the west. Rice valley and Tea
  Slopes to the south. Cherry Hills to the east, the Market Town at the crossroads
  south-east of the lake, and the coast with cliffs, harbour and the island along the
  whole eastern edge. Marshland where the main river meets the sea.
- **Rivers:** one main river from the snowy pass, through the waterfall, into Lantern
  Lake, then out through the marsh to the sea, plus 6–10 tributaries and many streams.
  Rivers are the natural "roads" of the world; following one always leads somewhere.
- **Vertical layers:** valley floors (fields, villages, rivers) → foothills (forests,
  shrines, tea) → mountain flanks (maple, cedar, waterfalls) → high ridges (pines,
  rocks, snow, views). Always offer a reason to climb: every high point has a
  viewpoint, a shrine or a secret.
- **Sightlines:** from every region you should see at least two other landmarks
  (castle keep, pagoda, lighthouse, volcano, the oldest cherry tree, the great
  waterfall's mist plume, the fire-watch tower). They help navigation without a minimap.
- **Points of interest density:** something interesting every 60–120 seconds of
  walking: a building, a shrine, an animal scene, a view, a wanderer, a secret.
  Generate a POI-density heatmap in the debug tools and fill empty areas.

### 6.16 Roads, paths & the life on them

  lanterns lighting up one by one at dusk.
- **Roadside stalls:** unmanned vegetable stands with an honesty box (leave a keepsake,

## 8. Player, movement & camera

### Movement
- **Walk** (default), **jog** (unlimited, no stamina), **slow walk** (crouch-walk:
  animals come closer), **jump** (modest), **climb** stairs, ladders and low ledges
  (auto-vault small steps ≤ 0.6 m), **wade** in shallow water (slower, ripples),
  **swim** calmly in deep water (no drowning, cannot dive deep), **slide** gently down
  snowy slopes for fun.
- **Sit** on any bench, step, dock edge, rock or cushion (context interaction): camera
  lowers, time-lapse option (§21).
- **Lie down** in grass/on a futon to look at the sky (camera tilts up; at night,
  shooting stars more likely to be noticed).
- **Rowboat:** enter at docks; row with left/right strokes or simple stick input;
  wake + ripples; can carry the lantern on the bow. Ferry between villages at set times.
- **No fall damage.** Steep cliffs simply can't be climbed; if you fall into a gorge,
  you land softly (fade + gentle landing) or float down in water.

### Camera
- First person, FOV 75° default (slider 60–100).
- **Subtle head bob** (toggle), **gentle sway** when idle (breathing), slight lean on
  turns, camera dips when landing.
- Hands/arms visible when holding items (lantern, fishing rod, camera, sketchbook),
  drawn in the same pixel style.
- Photo mode (§11) frees the camera nearby.

### Controls
- **Desktop:** WASD move, mouse look, Shift jog, Ctrl/C slow walk, Space jump,
  E interact, Q quick-use held item, Tab journal, M map, I inventory, P photo mode,
  F lantern on/off, Esc menu, mouse wheel cycle held item.
- **Touch (iPad):** left floating virtual stick (push further to jog), drag anywhere
  on the right half to look, contextual interact button that appears near
  interactables, jump button, small radial menu for items, two-finger tap opens
  journal, pinch on map. Large hit areas; nothing near the screen edges that conflicts
  with iPadOS gestures. Prevent double-tap zoom, scrolling, text selection.
- **Gamepad:** standard layout.
- All rebindable.

## 9. Interaction, UI & HUD

### Interaction
- Look at something interactable: a soft, small pixel icon/outline appears (no big
  text). Interactions: open/close, sit, lie, talk, pick up, examine (rotates object in
  a small view with a description), ring (bells), light (lanterns, incense), pray/offer
  (shrines), knock, feed (animals), pet (cats, dogs, deer bow back!), fish, row, read
  (signs, tablets, letters), photograph, sketch.
- **Examine text** is short, poetic and occasionally funny.

### HUD philosophy
- **Almost no HUD.** No compass, no minimap, no markers by default.
- Contextual elements fade in and out: interaction icon, held item, a tiny clock
  icon when the time changes period, discovery toast ("Discovered: The Weeping Pine")
  in brush-style text that fades.
- Optional toggles: compass strip, minimap, objective hints, subtitles for ambient
  voices.

### Menus (pause/journal)
- Styled like washi paper, ink brush accents, pixel art icons, soft page-turn sounds.
- Title screen: a slow camera drifting over Lantern Lake at night with the logo
  "HOTARU" fading in; "Wander" (continue) / "New Journey" (seed choice) / Settings.

### 9.4 Pixel-art UI (detailed, hand-crafted look)
**The whole UI is pixel art**, as detailed and lovingly made as the world. Every
panel, icon, button, font and cursor is made of crisp pixels. No smooth system
fonts, no default browser widgets, no blurry scaling, no flat "web app" look.

**Technical approach**
- Render the UI on its own **pixel canvas** (e.g. a 2D canvas or a Three.js
  orthographic layer) at a low base resolution (UI scale: one UI pixel = 3–5 screen
  pixels on iPad, chosen automatically as an integer, adjustable in settings), and
  upscale with nearest-neighbour. Use `image-rendering: pixelated` for any DOM
  fallback. UI pixels and world pixels should feel like the same artwork.
- A small retained-mode UI toolkit in `src/ui/`: panels, 9-slice frames, buttons,
  toggles, sliders, tabs, scroll lists (with momentum for touch), tooltips, text
  blocks with word wrap, icon grids, modal dialogs. Touch hit areas at least 44 pt
  even when the art is smaller.
- All UI art **generated in code** (pixel drawing helpers: lines, circles, dithered
  fills, outlines, 9-slice builders) or defined as small inline pixel maps
  (arrays/strings) in data files, so it stays offline and editable. Build a
  `ui-gallery` debug page that shows every UI element for review.

**Pixel fonts (made in code, bundled)**
- Design **2–3 original bitmap fonts**: a crisp readable body font (about 6×10 px
  glyphs, full Latin incl. German umlauts ä ö ü ß), a larger decorative heading font
  with a brush-stroke feel, and a tiny numeric font for HUD details.
- Proper kerning, line spacing, drop shadow / 1-px outline options, coloured inline
  words (item names, place names), and a few decorative glyphs (kanji-like original
  emblems, seals, leaf and lantern symbols).
- Text appears with a soft typewriter effect and tiny pixel sparkles on important
  words.

**Visual style**
- **Materials:** washi paper panels with pixel fibre texture and torn/deckled edges;
  dark lacquered wood frames with gold pixel highlights; indigo cloth (like a
  furoshiki) for menus at night; bamboo rods for scrolls; red hanko seal stamps.
- **Palette-aware:** the UI uses the same palette system as the world and shifts
  gently with time of day (warmer at sunset, cooler at night, lantern-lit glow in the
  dark).
- **Pixel icons for everything** (16×16 and 32×32 versions): every item, tool,
  keepsake, fish, insect, flower, feather, shell, animal, villager portrait, map
  stamp, weather type, moon phase, season, achievement. Hand-crafted look with
  1-px outlines, highlights and shading — not simple flat shapes.
- **Villager portraits:** 48×48 (or larger) pixel portraits for every villager and
  wanderer, generated from parts (face shape, hair, eyes, hats, clothing patterns,
  accessories) with several expressions (neutral, happy, surprised, sad, thinking,
  sleepy) shown in dialogue.
- **Animated details:** lantern icons flicker, paper corners flutter in wind,
  fireflies drift across the pause menu, ink drips on the journal title, a cat
  sleeps on the corner of the inventory and wakes up when you open it, petals or
  snow fall across menus depending on season, page-turn animations in the journal,
  stamps thump down with a little screen shake.
- **Cursor/selection:** pixel brush cursor on desktop; selection highlights as
  animated glowing ink outlines or a small firefly that hovers next to the chosen
  option.

**Screens (all pixel art)**
- **Title screen:** pixel logo "HOTARU" with kanji-style emblem, glowing lanterns,
  fireflies, a seal stamp, menu as wooden plaques hanging from strings.
- **Loading screen:** pixel lantern being lit + a haiku + a tiny progress bar shaped
  like a string of lanterns lighting up one by one.
- **HUD:** minimal: an interaction icon in a small paper bubble, the held item in a
  tiny lacquered frame, a sundial/moon-phase clock that appears on change, discovery
  banners as unrolling scrolls with brush-pixel text, gentle pixel subtitles.
- **Touch controls:** the virtual stick is a pixel-art stone/wood disc with a koi
  emblem; buttons are round lacquer tokens with pixel icons; semi-transparent and
  fading when not used.
- **Dialogue box:** washi panel with the speaker's pixel portrait, name on a wooden
  tag, text in the body font, choice buttons as little paper slips.
- **Inventory:** a wooden box with 12 compartments (like a bento/tansu drawer);
  items sit inside as detailed 32×32 icons; examining shows a large 64×64 version
  that slowly rotates in pixel 3D, with a description in brush font.
- **Journal:** a bound notebook with tab bookmarks (Places, People, Creatures,
  Flora, Secrets, Haiku, Album, Lore); pixel sketches, hanko stamps, pressed-flower
  art, handwritten-looking pixel text.
- **Map:** a large hand-painted pixel map on a scroll: pixel mountains, trees,
  villages and waves drawn in ink-and-watercolour pixel style; fog painted away as
  you explore; landmarks appear as little drawings; your own stamps.
- **Settings:** a wooden tansu with drawers for each category; sliders as beads on
  a string; toggles as tiny paper lanterns that light up when "on".
- **Photo mode:** pixel camera viewfinder frame with exposure ticks.

**UI sound** to match: soft paper rustles, wooden clicks, small bells, brush strokes,
stamp thuds, a koto pluck on confirm.

**Quality bar:** every screen should look like a screenshot from a carefully crafted
pixel-art game. Capture each screen with Playwright at iPad resolution and review it
before calling a milestone done.

## 12. Time, seasons & weather

### Day/night
- Full day = **24 real minutes** by default (setting: 12/24/48/real-time-synced).
- Periods: dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, golden hour, sunset, blue hour, night,
  deep night. Each with its own palette, ambience, NPC behaviours, animals.
- Transitions are slow and gorgeous; sunsets are a highlight (extra-long golden hour).

### Seasons
- Each season lasts **N in-game days** (setting: 3/7/14) — or the player can choose a
  season freely in settings ("eternal autumn" option!).
- **Spring:** cherry blossoms, petals, flooded rice terraces, koinobori, bush warbler
  songs, light rain, frogs waking.
- **Summer:** deep greens, cicadas, fireflies, thunderstorms, sunflowers, festival
  lanterns, fireworks, wind chimes everywhere, morning glories.
- **Autumn:** red/orange maples (reference 2), golden rice harvest, persimmons,
  pampas grass, harvest moon, crickets, leaf piles, mist.
- **Winter:** snow, ice-covered lakes in the north, steaming onsen, snow lanterns,
  camellias blooming red in snow, cranes dancing, frost on windows, quiet.

### Weather (state machine per region, transitions smoothly over minutes)
- Clear, partly cloudy, overcast, light rain, heavy rain, thunderstorm (distant
  lightning flashes, thunder delay based on distance, no danger), drizzle with
  sunshine (sunshower), fog/mist (morning valley mist, sea fog), windy, snow, heavy
  snow, blizzard-lite (soft, not dangerous), hail (rare, tiny), rainbow after rain,
  double rainbow (very rare).
- **Wind field:** global direction + strength + travelling gusts; affects grass,
  trees, cloth, banners, lantern strings (sway!), smoke, particles, water waves.
- **Wetness:** surfaces darken and become glossy in rain; drip lines from eaves;
  puddles form and slowly dry.
- **Snow accumulation** on rooftops, branches and terrain (shader-based, by normal),
  footprints in snow that slowly fill.
- Weather affects NPCs (umbrellas, staying inside, gathering under eaves), animals
  (hiding, frogs appear in rain), and sound.

## 19. Lore & the gentle story

An optional, quiet mystery you piece together, never forced:
- Long ago, the **castle Kagejō** was home to a young lord and his sister who loved
  the lake, the lanterns and the fireflies. One summer night, during the lantern
  festival, the sister disappeared into the lake mist following the fireflies.
  The lord had a thousand lanterns lit every night for years to guide her home.
  The castle fell silent, then into ruin.
- The lake village still lights lanterns every night "to guide someone home",
  though few remember who.
- Scattered **stone tablets**, letters, scrolls, castle murals, children's songs,
  and the masked stranger's gifts slowly reveal the story.
- **Ending (not a game end, just a moment):** after collecting enough pieces, on a
  summer night at the lantern festival, you float a special lantern; the fireflies
  gather into a path across the lake to the castle; the spirit of the sister appears,
  the masked stranger removes the mask, and the castle's inner garden blooms. The
  world continues afterwards, a bit brighter (more lanterns, more fireflies).
- Tone: melancholy but warm, about memory, guidance and home. Never dark or scary.

## 25. Audio & music (all procedural via Web Audio)

### Mixer
Buses: music, ambience, nature, voices, sfx, UI. Ducking (music lowers when
dialogue starts). Reverb per space (interior small room, temple hall, cave, forest,
open valley) via generated impulse responses. **Spatial audio** with HRTF panning
and distance/occlusion filtering (walls muffle, indoors sounds muffled).

### Music engine (adaptive, generative)
- Instruments synthesised in code: **koto** (plucked string via Karplus-Strong + body
  filter), **shakuhachi** (breathy filtered noise + sine with vibrato), **shamisen**
  (sharp plucked), **piano** (soft additive), **taiko** (for festivals), **music box**,
  **warm pads**, **bells/chimes**.
- Pentatonic/Japanese scales (yo, in, miyako-bushi) and gentle Western modes;
  phrase-based generative composition with motifs per region.
- Layers fade in/out by: region, time of day, weather, indoors/outdoors, discovery
  moments (a short "sting" when you discover a place), festivals (lively),
  night (sparse).
- **Silence is a feature:** music plays in arcs (2–4 min) separated by stretches of
  pure ambience.
- A recognisable **main theme** (lantern lake motif) that returns in variations.

### Ambience (layered, location-aware)
Wind (through grass, bamboo, pines, around castle walls), water (lake lapping, river

roofs, umbrella, water), thunder, birds (species-correct songs by time of day),

villages (murmurs, footsteps, chopping wood, cooking sizzles, children laughing,
doors sliding, dogs barking far away), temple bells, wind chimes (fūrin), bamboo

### SFX
Footsteps by surface (grass, gravel, wood boards, tatami, stone, sand, snow, mud,

## 28. Performance strategy & budgets

- **Frame budget (Medium, iPad M1):** ≤ 16.6 ms. Rough targets: draw calls < 400,
  triangles < 1.5 M visible, texture memory < 600 MB, JS heap < 500 MB.
- Rendering at low internal resolution is our big advantage — use it: expensive
  effects at low res, upscaled for free.
- Instancing everywhere; merge static meshes per chunk; frustum culling;
  distance culling per category; occlusion hints for interiors (portal-style: render
  interiors only when near/visible through doors).
- Worker-based generation with transferable buffers; main-thread upload budget per
  frame; chunk priority by view direction and distance.
- LOD + impostors for trees and buildings; far villages as merged low-poly meshes
  with emissive window dots at night (looks great from far).
- Light culling; capped shadow casters; update shadow cascades at staggered rates.
- Audio: limit active voices; virtualise far sources.
- Particle pools with hard caps per quality level.
- Monitor iPad Safari memory: avoid huge allocations, reuse geometry/buffers,
  dispose properly.
- Loading: first playable within ~10 s on iPad; show a beautiful loading screen
  (pixel lantern animation + haiku).

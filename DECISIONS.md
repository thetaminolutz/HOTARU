# Decisions

Non-obvious decisions and why. Newest at the bottom.

## Start (planning)

- **Hosting: GitHub Pages from a public repo** (owner's choice). Deploy workflow in M1.
- **Git flow: branch + PR per milestone** (owner's choice); Pages deploys from `main`.
- **iPad performance is measured by an in-game benchmark the owner runs on the iPad**,
  because this container renders with software GL and can't measure real fps.
- **Look beats size:** if 60 fps on Medium isn't reachable, shrink the world or merge
  minor regions before lowering rendering quality (owner's choice). World size is a
  single config constant.
- **No mid-M1 look checkpoint;** one stop at the end of M1 (owner's choice).
- **Default "New Journey" uses one fixed curated seed;** custom seeds are optional
  (owner's choice). Tuning and verification of landmarks and secrets target that seed.
- **Custom `ShaderMaterial`s instead of Three's PBR materials:** banded diffuse, tinted
  shadows, rim and fake subsurface need full control over the lighting model.
- **Palette quantization through a 3D LUT built in a worker:** an OKLab nearest-colour
  search per pixel over 96 colours is too expensive on the iPad GPU; a LUT is one fetch.
- **Point lights via a CPU-built screen-tile light grid:** the internal resolution is
  tiny (~640×360), so tiling on the CPU is cheap and needs no compute shaders.
- **`vite-plugin-pwa` (Workbox `generateSW`) for the service worker:** reliable precache
  manifest of hashed assets; the runtime is bundled, so no network at runtime.

## M1

- **Integer scale, no letterbox:** the internal resolution is chosen *from* the screen
  (`scale = round(screenH / targetH)`, internal = screen / scale, rounded up). iPad Air
  landscape at Medium → 472×328 at ×5. Pixels are always square and even, no filtering.
- **UI pixel = world pixel:** the UI canvas uses the same integer scale as the world, so UI
  and world read as one artwork.
- **Smooth pixel camera for first person:** camera *rotation* is snapped to whole-pixel
  angles and the remainder is applied as an image shift in the upscale pass (2-px render
  margin). Sub-pixel turns re-render nothing (stability test: 0 changed pixels). Position is
  not snapped — perspective parallax can't be stabilised and walking motion hides it.
  Everything that used to depend on camera *rotation* now depends on camera *position*
  (tree/grass billboards, water ripple axes in camera-centred polar coordinates). The dither
  pattern moves with the snapped rotation. The half-res reflection is snapped to whole
  reflection texels. Remaining difference after a one-pixel turn is ~10 % of water pixels
  (bloom downsampling + perspective), invisible in motion.
- **Palette blending:** all time-of-day palettes share the same 10 ramp slots × 7 shades
  (70 colours) so they blend colour-by-colour in OKLab. Palette colours update every frame;
  the 48³ nearest-colour LUT is rebuilt in a worker when the hour drifts by 0.2 h.
- **Single point-light list (32 nearest) instead of tiles for M1:** the dock has ~25 lights;
  the screen-tile light grid comes with villages in M7.
- **Fog in material shaders, not a post pass:** keeps reflections fogged correctly and costs
  nothing extra. Aerial colour = sky colour slightly above the horizon mixed toward the
  zenith → blue-lavender mountain layers (reference 2) instead of a warm haze. Distant
  backdrop ranges are separate meshes with flat per-layer haze (the "stepped" look).
- **Terrain light uses 75 % smooth Lambert, 25 % bands:** fully banded light on a heightfield
  shows the triangle grid; buildings, trees and props stay fully banded.
- **Forest on far mountains is painted by the terrain shader** (dark forest base + noise
  patches of autumn colour); instanced trees only within ~950 m, and their leaf cards grow
  with distance to stay ≥3 px (no speckle).
- **Water ripples in camera-centred polar coordinates** give horizontal streaks for every
  view direction (reference 1's broken golden reflections) while staying world-anchored.
- **Debug panel is plain DOM** (developer tool); everything player-facing is pixel art.
- **Heading font = body font upscaled with Scale2x + brush contrast** (thick downstrokes,
  tapered entries), logo = another Scale2x pass. One original design source, three sizes.
- **PWA via vite-plugin-pwa `generateSW`**, prompt mode: the toast "A new version is ready —
  tap to restart" applies the update only when tapped. Manifest and icons are hand-written /
  generated by `scripts/gen-icons.mjs` (pure Node PNG encoder).
- **npm `legacy-peer-deps`** (`.npmrc`): npm 10 crashes resolving vitest's optional peers.
- **Pages base path** comes from the repository name in the workflow, so the URL works
  whatever the repo's capitalisation.
- **Test scene, not the world:** M1 builds a fixed lake/dock scene (`src/scene/testWorld.ts`,
  `src/world/testTerrain.ts`) whose rules become the first draft of M2's world generator.

## After M1 (owner direction)

- **Autonomous build:** the owner asked me to continue through all milestones overnight
  without confirmations, merging each milestone PR myself once build, tests, screenshots
  and README are done. An hourly scheduled task resumes the session after usage limits.
- **New order:** M2 → M3 → M5 → M13 → M7 → M8 → M4 → M6 → M9 → M10 → M11 → M12 → M14
  (see PLAN.md §3). Audio, vegetation, interiors and fauna moved forward on request.
- **Apple Pencil:** Safari exposes pressure and tilt (pointerType "pen") but not the Pencil's
  double-tap/squeeze, and the iPad Air M1 has no hover. So: M3 makes the Pencil a precise
  pointer with its own pixel brush cursor; M11 adds pressure/tilt drawing (sketchbook,
  journal, map).

## M2

- **World = authored region map + noise:** 20 regions from SPEC §6/§6.15 (`src/world/data/regions.ts`)
  blend by domain-warped Gaussian weights. Mountains are broad squared-fbm massifs with soft
  ridges (pure ridged noise gave spires). Landmarks are sculpted on top: Lantern Lake (the M1
  shoreline, dock and islands kept exactly), the volcano cone and crater, the eastern sea with
  cliffs, the gull island, and border ranges outside the 10 km playable square (generated out
  to ±8 km so they're always on the horizon). Land outside the lake/sea is clamped above the
  water line so no accidental floods appear before hydrology (M4).
- **Quadtree terrain, 64×64 nodes:** four 8 km roots, nodes split by distance (split factor
  0.8 / 1.0 / 1.3 per preset), min node 128 m → 2 m cells at the player. A node is drawn only
  when all four children are ready (no holes, no overlaps). Skirts hide LOD cracks and face
  outwards (terrain is front-side only: from below, in the mirror, it must not be seen).
  64-cell nodes instead of 32 cut draw calls ~4× at equal detail.
- **Workers:** 1–3 terrain workers (hardware concurrency − 1) build nodes and tree tiles from
  the same pure height/biome functions as the main thread; jobs are prioritised nearest first.
- **Biome attributes per vertex** (forest, moisture, autumn, baked AO, grass tint, special
  feature) drive the terrain shader: meadow tint per region, forest floor near the camera vs.
  painted canopy beyond tree range, rice terraces with bunds, tea rows, volcanic sand,
  marsh, cliffs (tri-planar rock), snow line per region, beaches. Detail comes from a
  greyscale pixel texture array (8 × 32², 16 texels/m) tinted per material.
- **Trees stream in 256 m tiles** within 450 / 700 / 1000 m; tiles nearer than 220 m use the
  full cluster layout with trunks and shadows, farther tiles a third of the clusters (bigger)
  and no shadows. Beyond tree range, forests are painted on the terrain. Species mix per
  region (cedar, bamboo, cherry, ginkgo, birch added).
- **Reflections only near water:** the planar reflection re-renders the scene, so it runs only
  near Lantern Lake or the sea; elsewhere water shows the reflected sky (SPEC §3.8 fallback).
  Grass, trunks and far tree LODs are skipped in the mirror. The reflection camera has a 35 %
  wider frustum so rippled lookups never clamp at the edge (that produced vertical bars), and
  its rotation snaps to whole reflection texels.
- **Fog thins with altitude** (exponential height profile along the ray): valleys hazy,
  fly-overs clear, distant ranges as pastel layers.
- **Lighting:** baked per-vertex terrain AO, darker forest floors, moving cloud shadows on
  everything lit, 8 moon phases with moonlight scaled by fullness.
- **Debug:** world map with click-teleport, region list, POI-density heatmap (POI registry in
  `src/secrets/poi.ts`), per-pass draw counts, terrain/forest streaming stats.
- **Stability test runs with bloom off:** bloom re-samples on its own half-res grid, so a
  one-pixel turn changes bloom pixels; the core guarantee (sub-pixel turns change nothing) is
  unchanged and tested with everything on.

## M3

- **Controller as a small pose machine** (stand / air / wade / swim / slide / sit / lie) over
  the analytic heightfield and deck rectangles: walk 2.2 m/s, jog 4.8 (no stamina), slow 1.1;
  jump 4.4 m/s (≈0.75 m); step-up ≤ 0.6 m; slopes steeper than ~43° can't be walked up;
  wading below 0.3 m depth, swimming below 1.35 m (eye 0.35 m above the water, gentle bob,
  no diving, no drowning). Snow slopes > 0.35 slide gently. Falls never hurt: landing dips
  the camera, falls > 9 m end in a soft dithered fade.
- **Colliders are AABBs** for now (the boathouse); interiors open up in M7.
- **Interact = context first, then pose:** E / the interact token uses what you look at
  (sit spots on the pier and engawa so far); with nothing targeted it cycles sit → lie → stand
  ("sit anywhere", SPEC §8). Holding T / the hourglass token while sitting runs time ×20.
- **Camera feel:** head bob (toggle), idle breathing sway (also toggled by head bob, for
  comfort), a very slight lean into turns, landing dip. The breathing sway goes through the
  pixel-snapped rotation like any other look input, so it never shimmers.
- **Apple Pencil:** Safari reports the Pencil as `pointerType: "pen"` with pressure/tilt but
  not its double-tap/squeeze, and the iPad Air M1 has no hover. In play a Pencil *tap* turns
  the view smoothly toward that point and uses whatever is there; a *drag* looks with 2× the
  precision of a finger. A pixel brush cursor follows the Pencil (and the mouse in menus).
  Pressure drawing arrives with the sketchbook/journal/map in M11.
- **Gamepad (standard mapping):** left stick move, right stick look, A jump, X/B interact,
  Y hold time-lapse, LB/LT slow, L3/RT jog, Start menu. Touch: koi stick (gentle push = slow,
  rim = jog), drag right side to look, jump / interact / time-lapse tokens.
- **Rebinding:** every keyboard action is rebindable in Settings → Keys (stored with the
  settings in localStorage). Camera tab: FOV 60–100°, sensitivity ×0.25–2, invert Y, head bob,
  tap-to-jog.
- **Water mesh is now a camera-centred radial grid** (bug found in M3): the single 40 km quad
  from M2 lost varying precision when the eye was ~1.7 m above it, so close-up water rendered
  flat. Rings grow ×1.38 from 0.6 m to the horizon; the mesh follows the camera in 1 m steps.
- **Palette LUT race fixed:** a synchronous LUT rebuild invalidates any worker result still in
  flight, so an old hour's LUT can't overwrite the new one.

## M5

- **Grass everywhere = one instanced draw:** ground plants stream in 16 m tiles around the
  camera (35 / 55 / 80 m per preset), built in the terrain workers from the same biome
  functions, and are copied into a single instance buffer (one draw call for every plant on
  screen). 16 varieties share one 256×128 pixel atlas: greyscale parts take the region's
  grass tint per plant (greener where moist, golden where dry), coloured parts (cosmos,
  spider lilies, dandelions, cattail heads…) keep their colour. Plants shrink to nothing at
  the edge of the field and right in front of the camera (a 1 m card 50 cm away would
  magnify a texel into a block). They bend with wind gusts and away from the player.
- **Placement rules per biome:** reeds/cattails/horsetail in clumps at the water's edge, rice on
  terraces (tufts on the bunds), sasa under bamboo, pampas on the moor, ferns/moss/bellflowers on
  forest floors, meadows of tufts and clover with flower patches. The grass is skipped in the
  mirror (cost) and on decks.
- **Nine new species:** willow (new weeping shape), persimmon, camphor, hinoki, plum, zelkova,
  azalea, hydrangea, camellia, mixed into the regions where they belong (hydrangeas on Ajisaiyama,
  willows at the marsh and lake, persimmons in the rice valley…).
- **Deep forests:** the cedar, bamboo and temple woods get a second tree per placement cell
  (twice as dense). Draw calls don't grow (one draw per species per tile), triangles do.
- **Ambient particles:** one generic GPU effect (a single `Points` draw each) whose particles
  wrap in a box around the camera, so they stay world-anchored as you walk through them.
  Fireflies, leaves, petals, pollen, sun motes, rain, snow, sea spray, moths, plus local emitters
  (smoke, steam, sparks) that rise and drift downwind. Each effect's amount is set per frame from
  the region, the hour, the wind and (from M6) the weather; hidden effects cost nothing.
  Glowing points fade by brightness, soft ones (smoke, steam) by ordered dither.
- **Mist is fog, not sprites:** point-sprite mist banks read as dotted balls at this resolution.
  Ground mist is a second fog term in the shared fog function — dense near the ground, fading in
  over tens of metres — so trunks step back into layers (deep forests always, valleys and lakes
  in the morning). It eases in and out as you move between regions.

## M13 (audio, pulled forward)

- **Everything is synthesised with Web Audio, nothing is a recording** (offline, tiny download):
  koto and shamisen are Karplus-Strong strings rendered once per pitch in JS and cached;
  shakuhachi = sine + triangle with a delayed vibrato, a "meri" bend into each note and
  band-passed breath noise; soft piano and music box are additive partials; bells and wind
  chimes use inharmonic partials; pads are detuned saws through a low-pass; taiko is a pitch-
  dropping sine plus a noise thump.
- **Music in arcs, silence between (Minecraft-like):** one generated piece of 2–4 minutes, then
  2½–6 minutes of pure ambience ("occasional"; also often / rare / off in Settings → Sound). The
  first piece comes 25–60 s after you start; the title screen opens with the main theme.
- **Composer:** pure and deterministic (`composePiece(seed, mood)`), so it's unit-tested. Scales
  yo, in, miyako-bushi, ryūkyū and major/minor pentatonic; a mood per region and hour (forests
  get shakuhachi over koto in the *in* scale, villages koto and shamisen, the coast ryūkyū,
  flower hills music box, night sparse piano and music box). Sections A A′ B A″ (+ intro/coda),
  B built from a motif hashed from the region's name, A from the **Lantern Lake theme** at the
  lake, at dusk and on the title (in variations elsewhere). A short rising sting plays the first
  time you enter a region.
- **Ambience = beds + events.** Beds (wind low/high with gusts, leaf rustle tuned by autumn, pine
  and bamboo whoosh, lake lapping, surf swells, rain, a far night-insect chorus) follow place,
  wind and exposure. Events are Poisson-scheduled per minute from the same context and placed
  around the listener with HRTF panners and distance low-pass: bush warbler, tits, sparrows,
  turtle doves, crows, owls, woodpeckers, gulls, crickets, bell crickets, evening cicadas,
  frogs, kajika frogs, wind chimes at the dock, the temple bell at 6 and 18 h, bamboo knocks,
  shishi-odoshi, creaking cedar trunks, snapping twigs, dripping leaves, fish jumping. Dawn
  chorus in the morning, insects and frogs at night, gulls and surf at the coast.
- **Reverb per space** from generated impulse responses (open, forest, valley, room, hall, cave);
  two convolvers cross-fade when the space changes. Rooms/halls/caves get used from M7/M10.
- **Footsteps by surface** (grass, autumn litter, wood boards, gravel, sand, snow, shallow water,
  tatami ready for interiors), jump, landing thump by impact, swim strokes; the controller emits
  a step at the bottom of each head-bob cycle. UI buttons tick softly.
- **iOS:** the AudioContext is created on the first gesture and resumed when the app returns.
- **Listenable samples** are rendered offline (`npm run audio:samples` → `docs/audio/*.wav`)
  since nobody can hear this container.

## M7

- **Houses are generated, not modelled:** `buildHouse(spec)` builds walls with door and window
  openings, corner posts, a ceiling, a stone plinth to the ground, stone steps (≤ 0.28 m each)
  down from the door, and a roof — thatch (steep, thick, ridge battens), kawara tile (a sagging
  curve that lifts at the eaves), shingle or copper-green. Buildings turn in 90° steps so wall
  colliders stay axis-aligned boxes (the player's collision model).
- **Every house is enterable, no loading:** the interior is part of the building's merged mesh.
  Shōji doors slide open when you come within 2.6 m and close behind you. Interiors are
  furnished by type and profession (tatami, irori hearth with kettle, cushions you can sit on,
  chabudai, tansu, shelves, andon lamps, tokonoma with scroll and ikebana; potter's wheel,
  lantern-maker's lanterns, loom, carpenter's planks, fishing nets, an onsen bath with steam).
  Futons are laid out at night. Upstairs rooms are left for later (the M7 houses are one storey).
- **Interior light without new passes:** the shared shader knows the 8 nearest rooms (boxes); a
  fragment inside one gets 68 % less sky ambient and no rim light, while lamps and hearths glow
  there by day too. Outside walls aren't affected (their faces lie outside the room box). Point
  lights are uploaded by day only when a room is near.
- **Villages:** 7 sites (Ichiba market town, Inaho thatched farms, Chabatake tea hamlet,
  Shiomachi harbour, Sakuraoka, Yukemuri onsen village, Kumoji shrine). A winding main street
  follows the contour (plus a cross street in the market town), lots line both sides and face the
  street; a plaza with a well, lantern posts every ~18 m, a vermilion torii at the street's end.
  **The land under a village is graded** in the heightfield itself (a fifth of the relief kept),
  so terrain, workers, roads and placement all agree and houses don't stand on stilts.
- **Roads:** A* on a 24 m grid with a slope cost (roads wind over passes, avoid water — plank
  bridges if they must cross), Chaikin-smoothed, draped as dirt strips in 120 m chunks drawn
  within 650 m. Roads leave a village from the street end facing the next village.
- **Clearings:** house footprints, plazas and roads are sent with each tree/grass worker request
  (only the shapes touching that tile), so forests and grass keep clear of them.
- **Far villages:** beyond 480 m a village switches to one merged block-and-roof mesh plus a
  window mesh whose windows glow at night: two draw calls per village to 6 km.
- **Audio:** inside a house the reverb becomes a small room, the wind is sheltered, and
  footsteps sound like tatami; house floors and steps sound like wood.

## M8

- **41 species, one instanced draw each:** bodies are built from boxes in code (quadruped,
  bird, fish, insect, crab, tortoise, frog plans with per-species proportions, ears, antlers,
  tails, spots / stripes / masks / calico patches). Every vertex stores its part and pivot; the
  vertex shader swings legs with the gait (diagonal pairs), lowers the head to graze or bow,
  wags tails, folds or flaps wings, pitches flying birds and tucks the legs of sleeping
  animals. Hidden species cost nothing; the shadow pass uses the same animation.
- **Spawning by habitat:** 40 m cells within 120 m of the player, each deterministic per cell
  and two-hour window, so the same meadow has the same deer when you come back that morning.
  Habitats come from the world (forest, meadow, water, shore, marsh, coast, snow, village, farm,
  temple, onsen, flowers, sky) and each species' active hours; diurnal grazers sleep in place
  at night (deer lie in the grass), nocturnal ones (tanuki, foxes, owls, frogs) appear.
- **Behaviour:** idle, wander, graze, sleep, flee, approach, petted, bow; plus movement modes for
  circling kites and gulls, darting swallows, fluttering butterflies and dragonflies, perched
  owls, swimming ducks and koi, standing herons, hopping hares and sparrows.
- **Reactions:** jogging scares most animals (birds take off), walking scares them at half the
  distance, slow walking or standing still lets curious ones come up to you. Petting raises
  trust per species (shy animals flee less as trust grows). **Bowing:** temple deer can be bowed
  to — the camera dips in a bow and the deer bows back.
- **Calls:** cats, dogs, ducks, deer, chickens, horses, cows, macaques, kites, cranes, crows,
  gulls, owls, pigeons and frogs call from where they are (spatial audio).
- Ecosystem touches beyond habitat choice (herons hunting, crows following farmers, cats at
  the fish market) wait for villagers and water in M9/M4.
